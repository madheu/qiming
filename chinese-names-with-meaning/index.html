A Hànzi reference · 寓意 yùyì

Chinese names with meaning,
by theme.

A Chinese given name is built from one or two characters, and each character already means something. That is why asking what a Chinese name “means” has a real answer — unlike asking what “Michael” means, where the honest answer is usually etymology rather than sense.

How a character becomes a meaning

Two things are often confused. The literal meaning of a character is a dictionary entry — 明 is “bright”. The name meaning is what the choice of that character suggests about the person carrying it: clarity, judgement, a mind that does not stay cloudy.

Chinese parents choose characters for the second reason, not the first. That is also why a name can hold two characters that pull in different directions — a quality and its context — rather than simply stacking two pleasant words.

The examples below are illustrative, not a ranking. Each theme gives four given names built from the character in question. They show how the character behaves inside a real name; they are not a list of the most popular names in China, and popularity changes quickly.

Chinese names meaning light

明 is brightness as clarity — the opposite of confusion, not of darkness. 昭 is light made publicly visible, and 朗 is clear and open, as in a cloudless sky.

  • 明轩 Míngxuān — bright and unhurried
  • 晨曦 Chénxī — first light of morning
  • 光远 Guāngyuǎn — light that reaches far
  • 朗清 Lǎngqīng — clear and clean

Chinese names meaning moon

The moon is the quieter of the two great lights and carries associations of distance, patience and reunion. 玥 is a modern favourite: a legendary pearl, chosen largely for its sound and for sharing a reading with 月.

  • 望舒 Wàngshū — the charioteer of the moon, from classical poetry
  • 皓月 Hàoyuè — a bright moon
  • 月明 Yuèmíng — moonlight
  • 玥瑶 Yuèyáo — pearl and jade

Chinese names meaning sun

阳 is the sunny side and the active principle; 旭 is the sunrise; 昕 is the moment before dawn when light appears.

  • 旭尧 Xùyáo — sunrise over a high peak
  • 昕阳 Xīnyáng — dawn light
  • 朝阳 Zhāoyáng — the morning sun
  • 曦文 Xīwén — sunlight and pattern

Chinese names meaning star

星 is a star as a point of light; 辰 is the larger idea — a celestial body, a time marker, the fifth earthly branch. Names using 辰 tend to sound older and more formal.

  • 星辰 Xīngchén — the stars and the bodies they mark time by
  • 若星 Ruòxīng — like a star
  • 辰逸 Chényì — a celestial body at ease
  • 星遥 Xīngyáo — a distant star

Chinese names meaning jade

Jade is the classic Chinese image of virtue: hard, warm, and worked slowly. 玉 is the plain word, while 琳, 瑶 and 璐 are finer grades of the same idea.

  • 玉琳 Yùlín — jade of the finest kind
  • 瑶光 Yáoguāng — the star at the handle of the Big Dipper
  • 琳雅 Línyǎ — jade and refinement
  • 璐瑶 Lùyáo — two words for beautiful jade

Chinese names meaning lotus

The lotus grows out of mud and stays clean, which is why it stands for integrity rather than beauty alone. 荷 is the plant, 莲 the seed head, 蓉 the flower.

  • 清荷 Qīnghé — clear lotus
  • 莲心 Liánxīn — the lotus heart, slightly bitter and much respected
  • 荷风 Héfēng — wind over a lotus pond
  • 蓉华 Rónghuá — lotus in bloom

Chinese names meaning orchid

The orchid is the scholar's flower: it grows in seclusion and its fragrance is noticed without being advertised. 兰 is the common form; 蕙 is a rarer, more literary variant.

  • 兰亭 Lántíng — the Orchid Pavilion, site of a famous calligraphy gathering
  • 蕙心 Huìxīn — an orchid heart
  • 幽兰 Yōulán — the orchid in a quiet place
  • 兰芝 Lánzhī — orchid and a fragrant herb

Chinese names meaning phoenix

The 凤 phoenix is a bird of good order and high favour, traditionally paired with the dragon. It is used across genders, though it leans feminine in modern naming.

  • 凤仪 Fèngyí — the bearing of a phoenix
  • 鸣凤 Míngfèng — a phoenix calling
  • 凤鸣 Fèngmíng — the same image, reversed for sound
  • 凰玉 Huángyù — the female phoenix and jade

Chinese names meaning dragon

The Chinese dragon is a bringer of rain and good fortune, not a monster to be slain. 龙 is a bold character for a given name; many families prefer it in a compound such as 云龙 rather than alone.

  • 云龙 Yúnlóng — dragon among clouds
  • 龙飞 Lóngfēi — a dragon in flight
  • 子龙 Zǐlóng — a young dragon
  • 辰龙 Chénlóng — the dragon of the earthly branch

Chinese names meaning peace

安 is safety and settledness rather than mere quiet — a household at rest. 宁 adds calm, and 和 adds harmony between people.

  • 安宁 Ānníng — settled and calm
  • 予安 Yǔ'ān — peace, freely given
  • 静安 Jìng'ān — quiet and safe
  • 和光 Héguāng — light that has tempered itself

Chinese names meaning joy

悦 is inward gladness, 欣 is the lift of spirits, and 乐 is enjoyment as a way of living.

  • 欣怡 Xīnyí — glad and at ease
  • 悦然 Yuèrán — quietly pleased
  • 乐天 Lètiān — taking life gladly
  • 欢颜 Huānyán — a happy face

Chinese names meaning wisdom

智 is judgment in action, 慧 is insight, and 睿 is far-seeing intelligence — the one that sounds most formal.

  • 睿哲 Ruìzhé — far-seeing and wise
  • 智远 Zhìyuǎn — wisdom that reaches far
  • 思慧 Sīhuì — thoughtful insight
  • 明智 Míngzhì — clear judgement

Chinese names meaning strength

Chinese naming usually prefers steady strength to raw force: 毅 is resolve, 弘 is breadth, and 健 is health that keeps going.

  • 弘毅 Hóngyì — broad and resolute
  • 毅辰 Yìchén — resolve under the stars
  • 志强 Zhìqiáng — strong of purpose
  • 健行 Jiànxíng — walking on in good health

Chinese names meaning grace

雅 is refinement of manner, 婉 is gentle and indirect, 娴 is practiced ease. These lean feminine in modern usage but not exclusively.

  • 雅萱 Yǎxuān — refined, with a daylily
  • 婉清 Wǎnqīng — gentle and clear
  • 娴静 Xiánjìng — practiced and calm
  • 雅琴 Yǎqín — refinement and the zither

Chinese names meaning blessing or fortune

瑞 is an auspicious omen, 祥 is good fortune arriving, 福 is blessing in the plainest sense. These are warm, family-facing characters rather than guarded personal qualities.

  • 瑞祥 Ruìxiáng — auspicious signs together
  • 福临 Fúlín — blessing arriving
  • 祥和 Xiánghé — fortunate and harmonious
  • 嘉福 Jiāfú — an excellent blessing

Chinese names meaning snow

雪 is clean, quiet and a little austere — a winter character rather than a cold one. It pairs naturally with plants and weather.

  • 雪松 Xuěsōng — snow on the pine
  • 初雪 Chūxuě — the first snow
  • 雪梅 Xuěméi — plum blossom in snow
  • 雪晴 Xuěqíng — clearing after snow

Turning a meaning into a name

If you want to choose a name this way, the order matters:

  1. Pick the quality, not the word. “Moon” is an image; patience and distance are the qualities behind it. Choose the quality first and a character will follow.
  2. Check the character is usable in a name. Many meaningful characters are rare, too heavy, or read as a surname. 玥 is a common name character; 曦 is used but often feels grand.
  3. Read it beside a surname. A name that is elegant alone can collide with the family name's sound or tone. This is where most self-chosen names fail.
  4. Ask a fluent speaker. Tones and homophones are not visible in a table, and an unlucky overlap is the one thing you cannot fix later.

Questions about names and meanings

Do Chinese names have meanings?

The characters do. A Chinese given name is assembled from one or two characters, and those characters carry meaning, so the name carries that meaning with it. It is not a translation of an English word — the association comes from the characters chosen.

Are these the most popular Chinese names?

No. They are illustrative given names built from the character in each theme, chosen to show how the character is used in practice. Popularity shifts quickly and by region, so treat this as a tour of meanings rather than a ranking.

Can I pick a Chinese name for its meaning?

Yes, and it is one of the two main routes. Start from the quality or image you want to carry, choose characters that express it, then check that the result sounds like a name rather than a phrase. A fluent speaker should check tones and unwanted homophones.

What about names with darker meanings?

Search results include requests for names meaning death, shadow or chaos, but those are usually for fiction and games rather than Chinese naming. Everyday Chinese naming tends toward light, harmony, virtue and nature; a name that reads as sinister on a real person is unusual.
