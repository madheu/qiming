02 / The twelve-year circle

Meet the Chinese zodiac animals.

The order stays the same: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig. The examples below identify the animal year that begins during each Gregorian year; they do not cover every birthday within that year.

03 / The detail that matters

The year does not begin on January 1.

For this guide, an animal year starts on Chinese New Year—the first day of the first month of the Chinese lunisolar calendar. This date changes each Gregorian year, usually falling in January or February. A person born before that date belongs to the preceding animal year.

For example, the Horse year begins on February 17, 2026. February 16 is still in the Snake year. The Horse year continues through February 5, 2027; the Goat year begins on February 6.

How to confirm: look up your birth year in the Hong Kong Observatory's Gregorian–Lunar calendar conversion tables, find the first day of the first lunar month, and compare your birthday with that date. This calculator uses that Lunar New Year convention, not the Start of Spring (Lìchūn) boundary used by some astrological systems.

04 / A few good questions

Chinese zodiac FAQ

How do I find my Chinese zodiac sign?

Start with your birth year in the calculator or table. For an exact Lunar New Year boundary check, provide your month and day too—especially if your birthday is in January or February.

Is the zodiac the same as Western astrology?

No. This animal cycle is based on years in the Chinese calendar, rather than Western astrology's twelve sun signs. More elaborate Chinese systems also consider months, days, hours, and the five phases; this page is an introduction to the animal year only.

Does my animal decide my personality or future?

No. Animal symbolism is cultural material, not scientific evidence about personality or a reliable forecast. Use it as a conversation starter, not a basis for health, financial, or relationship decisions.

Should my Chinese name match my zodiac animal?

It does not have to. Some naming traditions consider birth information, but a natural Chinese name can be chosen for its sound, meaning, and personal resonance without referencing your zodiac sign.
