A Hànzi reference · 姓氏 xìngshì

Chinese surnames,
in order.

Chinese names put the family name first. This page lists the 100 surnames you are most likely to meet, with the character, its Mandarin reading, what the character literally means, and where the surname is usually said to come from.

Why the surname comes first

A Chinese full name is written family name first, then given name: 林知远 is Lín (surname) + Zhīyuǎn (given name). The word for a full name is 姓名, literally “surname-name”, which makes the hierarchy explicit.

Two things follow from that. First, Western forms can mislead you: writing “Zhīyuǎn Lín” is only for international contexts, not for Chinese. Second, when someone introduces themselves formally, they may name their family first as a matter of course, not as emphasis.

A small number of surnames use two characters — 欧阳, 司马, 诸葛. Never split them into a surname and a given name.

Before you read the table: two cautions

Character meaning is not surname origin. 王 literally means “king”, but the surname does not mean “someone was a king”; it is generally traced to descendants of royal lines. The table below separates the character’s literal meaning from the commonly cited origin, and leaves the origin blank where the account is disputed.

Rankings are approximate. Population rankings differ by source, survey year and whether Taiwan, Hong Kong and overseas communities are counted. Treat the order after the top few as indicative, not exact.

Why the same surname has several English spellings

Mainland Chinese names use Hanyu Pinyin: 张 is Zhang, 陈 is Chen. But many overseas Chinese families left Guangdong, Fujian or Hong Kong generations ago, and their documents carry Cantonese or Hokkien romanisations instead. That is why one surname family can appear under several English forms.

Two-character surnames

Questions about Chinese surnames

What is the most common Chinese surname?

王 (Wáng) is usually ranked first in population surveys, with 李 (Lǐ) and 张 (Zhāng) close behind. Exact rankings shift between sources and years, so treat any precise order beyond the top few as approximate.

Why does the surname come first in Chinese names?

Chinese names are ordered family first, then individual, so a full name reads as surname plus given name. The word for full name is 姓名, literally “surname-name”, which shows the same priority.

How are Chinese surnames written in English?

Mainland Chinese names are romanised in Hanyu Pinyin, for example 张 as Zhang and 陈 as Chen. Many overseas Chinese families use older dialect spellings instead, so the same surname appears as Cheung, Chan, Wong or Ng depending on family history.

Are there two-character Chinese surnames?

Yes. A small number of compound surnames use two characters, such as 欧阳 (Ōuyáng), 司马 (Sīmǎ), 上官 (Shàngguān) and 诸葛 (Zhūgě). They are uncommon but well documented and should never be split.

Is the Hundred Surnames poem a popularity ranking?

No. The classic 百家姓 text was composed in the Song dynasty, and its opening — 赵钱孙李 — reflects political favour at the time rather than population. Use a modern survey if you need actual frequency.
