A Hànzi comparison · 名 vs 字 vs 号

Chinese name
vs courtesy name.

English often uses “name” for several different naming roles. Traditional Chinese culture gives those roles different words—and different social jobs.

名: the given name

Your 名 is the given name people use in ordinary introductions and daily life. In this site’s main generator, the Chinese family name comes first, followed by the given name.

字: the courtesy name

Your 字 is an additional traditional name. Historically, it was associated with adulthood and used among peers, friends, students, and intellectual circles. It was related to the 名 through meaning, contrast, an extended image, or a classical reference.

It is not normally used for a passport, a bank account, or a modern official form. For a foreign learner, think of it as a culturally meaningful peer-facing name.

号: the art or studio name

Your 号 is different again. It is often self-chosen and expresses a place, ideal, artistic identity, or literary mood. Historical examples include 李白’s 青莲居士 and 苏轼’s 东坡居士. The suffix 居士 has Buddhist meaning, so it should not be added casually to someone’s name.

This Hànzi release generates a 字 only. It explains 号 so that foreign users do not confuse an art name with a courtesy name.
