How to choose a
Chinese courtesy name.

A good courtesy name does not float separately from your name. It answers it, extends it, or gives it a second angle.

1. Start with the given name

Look at the meaningful character in your given name. Is it about brightness, calm, distance, courage, water, learning, or another quality? The 字 should have something to say about that character.

2. Choose a relationship

Same meaning

Use a related but more classical character. 明 may lead to 昭: both carry light, but 昭 feels more formal.

Complementary contrast

Pair a quality with its inward or opposite half. 明 may lead to 晦: light is named, darkness is held as its counterweight.

Semantic extension

Follow the image outward. 知 may lead to 远: understanding becomes a horizon.

Classical allusion

Use a relationship supported by a known text or historical naming pattern. If the source is uncertain, do not label it an allusion.

3. Pick a form

子X is an accessible form for a modern cultural exercise. A form such as X之 feels more literary. A ranking prefix such as 伯, 仲, 叔, or 季 should only be used when the family order is actually known.

4. Say it aloud

Check the Mandarin pinyin and listen to the rhythm beside your full Chinese name. Tone marks are part of the pronunciation, even when English speakers find them unfamiliar.

Use the right context: a courtesy name is not a passport name. It is a cultural or social form, especially suitable for writing, study, friendship, historical role-play, or a creative identity.
